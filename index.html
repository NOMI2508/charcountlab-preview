    How this character counter works

    Type or paste into the box and every number updates as you go. There is no button to press and nothing is sent to a server: the counting is done by your own browser.

    • Characters is the number of characters a reader would see. Every letter, digit, space, punctuation mark and line break is one. So is every emoji, even one that is built from several parts.
    • Characters without spaces is the same count with all whitespace left out: ordinary spaces, tabs, line breaks and non-breaking spaces.
    • Words are found with the browser’s language-aware word rules, not by splitting on spaces, so contractions and hyphenated words are counted the way a person would count them.
    • Sentences, paragraphs and lines sit just below the main numbers. A paragraph is a block of text separated from the next by a blank line.

    The exact rule behind each number, with worked examples, is on How We Count.

    What is a character counter?

    A character counter tells you how long a piece of text is, measured in characters rather than words or pages. That matters whenever a box, a field or a platform stops accepting text at a fixed length: a bio, an ad headline, an application answer, a product title, a text message.

    A good counter also has to be clear about what it is counting, because “length” has more than one meaning. The same text can honestly be described as 1 character, 7 code points, 11 code units or 25 bytes. This one shows the number a person would expect first and keeps the technical measurements one click away.

    Check your text against a limit

    Enter any number in the Character Limit field to see how much you have used, how much is left and the percentage. If you go over, the counter tells you by how much; it never stops you typing. On a phone, the remaining figure stays visible beside the main count while you write.

    You can also pick a preset. Each one is labelled with what kind of number it is:

    • Platform limit is a maximum the platform itself enforces and documents.
    • Common guideline is an editorial rule of thumb. Search result titles and descriptions belong here: Google publishes no character limit for either, and what gets cut off depends on the width of the text on the searcher’s screen.

    What counts as a character?

    For almost every limit you will meet, the answer is: everything you can type. Letters, digits, spaces, full stops, commas, quotation marks, symbols such as @ and #, and line breaks each count as one character.

    The surprises come from things you cannot see. Text copied from a word processor or a web page can carry non-breaking spaces, zero-width characters and typographic quotation marks. They look harmless and still count. If a number seems too high, open Advanced statistics and compare “Whitespace characters” and “Other” with what you expect.

    What counts as a character, in detail

    Characters with and without spaces

    Both conventions are in everyday use. Social platforms, form fields and most “maximum length” rules count spaces. Some publishers, translators and academic briefs ask for a count that excludes them. When a requirement only says “characters”, it nearly always means with spaces; when it matters, ask.

    Here, “without spaces” removes every kind of whitespace, not only the space bar. A text with ten line breaks is ten characters shorter without spaces than a text that merely has its spaces deleted.

    When to use each count, with examples

    Why emoji and accents make counting complicated

    Computers store text as numbers, and one visible character can be stored as several of them. A thumbs-up with a skin tone is two code points. A family emoji is four people glued together with three invisible joiners. An accented letter may be one code point or a plain letter followed by a separate accent.

    Software that counts the stored pieces instead of the visible result reports a larger number. That is why a message that looks 10 characters long can be rejected by a field that allows 20. These rows are measured by the same code the counter above uses:

    Character count or word count?

    Word counts suit long writing, where the reader’s time is what matters: essays, articles, reports. Character counts suit anything that has to fit a space: a field, a screen, a headline, a message. The two do not convert reliably, because word length varies with language, subject and style, so use the measure your requirement is written in.

    What people use it for

    • Posts, bios and captions that have to fit a platform’s maximum.
    • Application forms and personal statements with a strict character allowance, where every space counts.
    • Page titles and meta descriptions, using the common guidelines as a sanity check rather than a rule.
    • Ad headlines and descriptions, which are short enough that one word decides whether the copy fits.
    • Interface text and product data: button labels, notification copy, titles that must not overflow a layout or a database column.
    • Translation and editing, where work is often scoped by characters.

    Counting a text message

    Writing a text message? Use the SMS counter

    SMS does not count like other text. One emoji or curly quote can cut a message’s capacity from 160 to 70 and split it into several billed segments. The SMS Character Counter shows the encoding, the segments and the characters responsible.

    Your text stays on your device

    The counter is a small program that runs inside this page. What you type is read, counted and forgotten in your browser’s memory. It is not uploaded, not logged, not stored and not included in any analytics event. Only your settings, such as a chosen limit, are saved in your browser so they are still there next time.

    Like any website, the site itself is delivered by a web server that keeps standard access logs, and it may use analytics or advertising services that process information about the visit. None of that includes the text you enter. The privacy policy sets out exactly what is and is not collected.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does the character count include spaces?

    +

    Yes. “Characters” counts every space, tab and line break along with letters, digits, punctuation and emoji. “Characters without spaces” is the same count with all whitespace left out. Both numbers are shown side by side so you can use whichever your requirement asks for.

    Counting with and without spaces

    Does an emoji count as one character?

    +

    In the main Characters figure, yes: one emoji is one character, including flags, skin-tone variants and family emoji that are built from several parts. Other systems count differently. JavaScript, many form fields and SMS count UTF-16 units, where the same emoji can be 2, 4 or 11. Open Advanced statistics to see every unit for your text.

    Why emoji counts differ

    Why do two character counters disagree?

    +

    They are usually measuring different things. The common causes are emoji and accented letters (visible characters versus code units), line breaks (counted as one character, two, or not at all), whether spaces are included, and invisible characters picked up by copying from a document. The Advanced panel shows visible characters, code points, UTF-16 units and UTF-8 bytes together, so you can see which one the other tool matches.

    Common counting mistakes

    Is my text uploaded?

    +

    No. Counting happens in your browser with JavaScript. The text you type or paste is not sent to a server, is not saved, and is not included in analytics. Only your settings, such as the limit you chose, are remembered in your browser’s local storage.

    Privacy policy

    What is the difference between characters and bytes?

    +

    A character is something you see; a byte is a unit of storage. In UTF-8, the usual encoding on the web, an unaccented English letter takes 1 byte, most accented letters take 2, most Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters take 3, and most emoji take 4. Limits on database fields, file sizes and some APIs are set in bytes, so check the UTF-8 bytes figure in Advanced statistics when that is the unit you need.

    Bytes versus characters

    Do line breaks count as characters?

    +

    Yes, each line break counts as one character and is excluded from “Characters without spaces”. Text copied from Windows often stores a line break as two characters (carriage return plus line feed); the text box converts it to one. If a form you are filling in counts line breaks as two, allow one extra character per line break.

    How whitespace is counted

    How are words counted?

    +

    Words are found with the browser’s built-in word segmentation rather than by splitting on spaces. Contractions such as “don’t” are one word, numbers such as 3.14 are one word, hyphenated compounds such as “well-known” are one word, and emoji and punctuation are not words. Languages written without spaces, such as Chinese and Japanese, are split with the browser’s dictionaries.

    Full counting method

    Can I check my text against a platform’s limit?

    +

    Yes. Under “Check Against A Limit” you can type any number or pick a preset. Presets are labelled either “Platform limit”, for a maximum the platform enforces, or “Common guideline”, for editorial rules of thumb such as search title length. Only X publishes exactly how it counts, so X posts use its weighted rules and the other presets are marked as approximate.
